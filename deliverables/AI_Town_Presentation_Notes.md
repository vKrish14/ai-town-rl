# AI Town presentation notes

Presenter notes follow the 24-slide deck in order. Values and equation details are implementation-specific. All saved comparison results cited below describe population 10, while the current viewer contains 17 citizens.

## Slide 1: AI Town: A Living Autonomous Society

Open with the key distinction: AI Town is a custom synthetic multi-agent environment with a Pygame viewer. Learned policies select high-level life activities, the environment applies transitions, and the graphical layer displays the resulting state. The screenshot is generated by the running project, not an illustrative mock-up. Course, team, institution and guide fields were left out because the repository supplied none.

Sources: README.md; results/academic_town_screenshot.png

## Slide 2: Problem statement and research question

The project studies how several autonomous citizens can choose daily activities while sharing finite resources and affecting one another. A citizen must balance personal needs such as hunger, energy, health, stress and social satisfaction. Work, food purchases and hiring connect decisions through a shared economy. Socialising can also change nearby citizens’ wellbeing.

The central question is: can a learned shared policy produce sustainable life choices in this coupled environment? This is a controlled simulation question, not a claim that synthetic citizens reproduce real human behaviour.

Sources: README.md; docs/RL Formulation.md

## Slide 3: Project objectives and system architecture

The project has separate simulation, environment, learning, evaluation and graphics modules. `Town` owns citizen state and transitions. `TownParallelEnv` exposes per-citizen observations and actions through PettingZoo’s Parallel-style step interface. The training script collects episode rollouts, computes GAE, updates a shared actor and selected critic, and saves a checkpoint with metadata. Evaluation loads that checkpoint and writes episode JSON. The app calls the policy interface and renders state. It does not calculate rewards or make citizens’ life choices.

Sources: docs/Architecture.md; ai_town/environment.py; train.py; evaluate.py; ai_town/app.py

## Slide 4: Town simulation and clock

Current Willowbrook uses a 50 × 32 map with 17 named citizens. It includes homes, four offices, three groceries, two restaurants, two clinics, three parks, a town centre and a safety office. One environment tick represents one simulated hour. The configured training/evaluation episode is 30 days, or 720 hours at 24 ticks per day. The interactive viewer runs open-ended. At the current 1× viewer setting, one simulated hour takes one real second.

The main result files discussed later used an earlier population-10 configuration and an earlier map. The screenshots show the current 17-citizen town; they are not the state under which those old metrics were collected.

Sources: config/default.yaml; ai_town/buildings.py; ai_town/environment.py; README.md; results/post_navigation_fix/summary.csv

## Slide 5: Mathematical model: partially observed multi-agent stochastic game

A precise description is a finite-population partially observable stochastic game with coupled transitions and individual rewards. At each hour, each citizen receives local observation o_i, selects an action, and the simulator advances the shared town. The user’s requested Markov-game tuple is useful if the global state s_t includes all simulator state required to make transitions Markov. The implementation’s critic input is narrower: it concatenates the local observations, so call it the concatenated global observation vector rather than the complete physical simulator state.

The town’s shared economy and social effects create coupling. Each citizen still receives an individual scalar reward.

Sources: docs/RL Formulation.md; ai_town/environment.py; ai_town/simulation.py

## Slide 6: Citizen state and 28-value observation

The citizen object stores six bounded needs, money, employment, occupation, food inventory, four personality values, location, action-in-transit state and histories. The actor receives only a 28-element float32 observation. Elements 0–5 are health, energy, hunger, happiness, stress and social, each divided by 100. Money is min(money/200, 1), followed by employed flag, normalized hour, capped normalized day, x/35, y/23, minimum shop stock/40, vacancies/4 capped at one, food/5 capped at one, mean Manhattan distance to others/50, four traits and an eight-entry pending-action one-hot.

A material interface issue is visible in code: the expanded map is 50 × 32, while x and y still divide by 35 and 23. Some position features can exceed 1 even though `observation_space` declares Box(0,1). This deck reports the implemented behavior and lists it as a fix.

Sources: ai_town/simulation.py:585-625; ai_town/environment.py:35-37; ai_town/buildings.py; config/default.yaml

## Slide 7: Action space and citizen decision cycle

The shared actor produces logits over eight discrete high-level activities. In stochastic training and normal evaluation it samples from a categorical distribution; deterministic evaluation takes the largest probability. If a citizen is travelling to a committed activity, the environment returns a mask that leaves only that action available. The actor sets masked logits to −1e9 before constructing the categorical distribution. Resource and destination rules are enforced by the simulator, not by a complex action mask.

Path planning uses deterministic A* as a movement aid. It chooses where to walk after the life activity is selected; it does not decide which life activity to take.

Sources: ai_town/simulation.py:20-29; ai_town/environment.py:66-79; ai_town/rl.py:74-89; ai_town/navigation.py; docs/RL Formulation.md

## Slide 8: Environment transition mechanics

One step is an hour. TownParallelEnv receives one action per agent, and Town.step_agent applies each citizen’s movement and activity. Positions advance up to eight path cells per hour in the current configured app/training setup. Need drift is then applied: energy −1.4, social −0.35, stress +0.1, hunger +0.7. Hunger above 80 reduces health by 0.8; energy below 15 reduces health by 0.45. Needs are clamped to [0,100].

Activity examples: sleep adds 24 energy and 1.5 health, then ordinary hourly costs apply; exercise adds 3 health but costs 8 energy and 5 hunger before drift; socialising adds 18 social, 7 happiness and reduces stress by 4, and nearby peers can receive social/happiness benefits. Salary pays half the configured 12-dollar salary when the work cadence condition is met, rather than every hour.

Sources: ai_town/simulation.py:296-493; ai_town/environment.py:82-106; config/default.yaml

## Slide 9: Reward function and worked example

For a living citizen, W=(health+energy+(100−hunger)+happiness+(100−stress)+social)/6. The implemented reward is .05ΔW − .30 max(0,hunger′−50)/50 + .08 I[sustainable work] + .15(min(money′,60)−min(money,60))/60 + .25 I[newly employed]. If the citizen is dead after the transition, the final batch reward is only −.50. Sustainable work requires employed, money below 50, selected Work action and arrival.

Worked Eat example (constructed from actual rules, not an observed episode): before needs (80,60,50,65,20,45), carried food 1, at home. Eat reduces hunger by 35; ordinary drift adds 0.7. Energy, social and stress apply ordinary hourly changes. The wellbeing score rises from 63.33 to 68.74, ΔW=5.408. Other components are zero. Reward=.05×5.408=.2704. The example illustrates the code, not a measured policy result.

Sources: ai_town/rewards.py; ai_town/simulation.py:251-290,396-489; config/default.yaml

## Slide 10: Reinforcement learning objective

A policy maps observations to a distribution over actions. A trajectory is a sequence of observations, actions, rewards and termination signals. An episode is one finite 30-day run during training/evaluation. Discounted return weights near-term reward more than distant reward. Here γ=.99. On-policy updates collect fresh rollouts with the current policy, then update parameters to increase estimated return. Sampling gives exploration; choosing argmax is used only for deterministic diagnostics.

Sources: docs/RL Formulation.md; ai_town/rl.py; train.py

## Slide 11: PPO clipped policy objective

PPO reuses a collected on-policy rollout for several minibatch epochs. For each recorded action, the probability ratio compares the new policy with the behavior policy that generated the action. The advantage says whether that action produced more return than the value baseline predicted. Clipping the ratio to [1−ε,1+ε] limits the surrogate’s incentive for a very large update; it does not guarantee a hard trust region. The implementation maximizes the clipped objective, represented in code as minimizing its negative, and subtracts entropy to encourage exploration.

Sources: Schulman et al. 2017: https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.06347; ai_town/rl.py:219-244

## Slide 12: Temporal-difference error and generalized advantage estimation

The critic estimates expected future return. For a transition, δₜ=rₜ+γ(1−dₜ)Vₜ₊₁−Vₜ is the one-step TD residual. GAE sums discounted residuals with factor γλ to trade variance against bias. With λ=0.95, it mixes multiple horizons. The return target is Âₜ+Vₜ. The code computes this backward across the episode, using zero next value at the final step and multiplying by the nonterminal mask. Episodes finish at the configured day limit with terminations; the environment reports truncations false. The code therefore does not bootstrap past the finite episode boundary.

Sources: Schulman et al. 2016: https://arxiv.org/abs/1506.02438; ai_town/rl.py:323-335; ai_town/environment.py:93-105

## Slide 13: Multi-agent learning and parameter sharing

Each citizen has a separate observation, action, reward and state trajectory, but all citizens use the same actor parameters. This reduces the number of policies to train and fits homogeneous citizens with a common eight-action interface. Personality features allow different local inputs to produce different actions. Shared parameters do not mean identical actions, nor a shared team reward. Socialising can alter nearby peers and a shared food inventory couples purchasing outcomes.

The decision rules remain individual; environmental effects couple their consequences. The police citizen also uses the same actor and reward, with Work routed to the Safety Office.

Sources: docs/RL Formulation.md; ai_town/simulation.py; ai_town/environment.py

## Slide 14: MAPPO: centralized training, decentralized execution

MAPPO uses a shared decentralized actor and a centralized value critic during training. In this code, the critic input is the ordered concatenation of all citizen observations, shape [N×28]. At the current N=17, that vector has 476 values. The critic output has one value per citizen, shape [17]. It is not a separate complete world-state object: it is built by concatenating the same per-citizen observation vectors.

During deployment, each actor call uses only that citizen’s 28 local features. The critic is not needed to select an action. Existing checkpoints trained under population 10 contain a population-shaped critic with 280 inputs and 10 outputs; metadata records the training population.

Sources: docs/RL Formulation.md; ai_town/rl.py:28-44,62-73; ai_town/simulation.py:621-627; docs/Architecture.md

## Slide 15: MAPPO optimization objective and implementation

The actor uses the same clipped PPO surrogate as PPO. The critic predicts one value per citizen from the concatenated observation state. The implementation minimizes critic coefficient 0.5 times mean squared error between predicted values and GAE return targets. Actor entropy coefficient is 0.01 and is subtracted from the minimized actor loss, so it encourages higher policy entropy. Actor and critic use separate Adam optimizers. Gradient norms are clipped to 0.5. The code performs actor and critic minibatch updates separately, rather than one monolithic joint loss.

Sources: ai_town/rl.py:183-250; docs/RL Formulation.md; Yu et al. 2022: https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/9c1535a02f0ce079433344e14d910597-Abstract.html

## Slide 16: Training pipeline and software implementation

The training script creates a headless PettingZoo-compatible environment, resets with a seed, collects one full episode and stores observations, concatenated states, actions, old log probabilities, action masks, rewards, values and done flags. It computes GAE, normalizes the advantages inside the update, uses four epochs with minibatches of at most 64, then writes checkpoint metadata. For the documented results, training commands used 100 episodes × 30 days. The CLI default is 20 episodes if --episodes is omitted. Current configuration uses CPU threads=1, learning rate 0.0003, γ=.99, λ=.95, clip=.2 and entropy=.01. Actor/critic networks each use two 128-unit Tanh hidden layers.

Sources: train.py; ai_town/rl.py; config/default.yaml; docs/Testing and Experiments.md

## Slide 17: Dataset generation, storage and provenance

There is no external human-behaviour dataset bundled with the repository. AI Town generates its own training experience online through seeded simulator interactions. A stored rollout transition is conceptually (oₜ,aₜ,rₜ,oₜ₊₁,dₜ) for each citizen, plus current action mask and centralized critic input during training. For one 30-day, 17-citizen episode, the observation tensor is [720,17,28], actions [720,17], and global critic input [720,476]. The rollout arrays are assembled in memory and are not saved as one canonical dataset file. Training diagnostics are saved as JSONL; evaluation episodes and metadata are saved as JSON; model weights and training metadata are saved as checkpoints.

Sources: train.py:47-115,164-169; evaluate.py:52-119; README.md; results/post_navigation_fix/summary.csv

The retained post-navigation-fix comparisons use the default evaluation file config: 10 citizens, 30 days, and 15 environment seeds (1001–1005 and 7001–7010). They compare random, rule and three independent PPO checkpoints (training seeds 29,129,229) and three MAPPO checkpoints (23,123,223). The project’s current config has 17 citizens, so these results do not represent the current town layout. Policies sample categorical actions in primary evaluation.

## Slide 19: Recorded outcomes: wellbeing and survival

Chart values are mean final town wellbeing for each retained checkpoint, evaluated for 30 days on 15 environment seeds. The x-axis names distinguish independent training seeds. Random and Rule are transparent baselines, not learned policies. Survival percentages beside the chart come from the same summary file. Results used population 10, older than the current 17-citizen map. Across the three training seeds, PPO final wellbeing ranged 67.63–73.96 and survival 98.0–98.7%. MAPPO wellbeing ranged 88.01–89.59 and survival 98.0–100%. Three training seeds and 15 environment seeds per checkpoint are informative but limited; no significance test or claim of universal algorithm superiority is made.

Sources: results/post_navigation_fix/summary.csv; docs/Testing and Experiments.md

## Slide 20: Robustness and reward ablation evidence

High-food-price stress test: 10 matched environment seeds (9001–9010), 30 days each, using fixed checkpoints and a stress configuration. Survival was Random 0%, Rule 100%, PPO 97%, MAPPO 98%; wellbeing 53.37, 66.80, 66.62, 88.69, respectively. This measures variation across environment seeds for those checkpoints, not training-seed uncertainty.

Financial-stability ablation: MAPPO with that reward weight set to zero, three training seeds and five evaluation seeds per checkpoint. Full reward survival was 100% for all three seeds. Ablated survival was 100%, 100%, 20%; wellbeing changes averaged −19.83 points across training seeds, with sample SD 17.70. The response is seed-sensitive and exploratory, not a stable causal estimate.

Sources: results/economy_stress_expanded/summary.json; results/ablations/financial_stability_three_seed_summary.json; docs/Testing and Experiments.md

## Slide 22: Limitations and next research steps

The largest reporting limitation is population/configuration drift: the visible town now has 17 citizens, while retained main comparisons use population 10 and old map coordinates. Current-town evaluation should be regenerated before making claims about the 17-citizen layout. The observation Box also declares [0,1], although expanded-map position divisions x/35 and y/23 allow values above 1. That interface should be corrected and old checkpoints reevaluated.

Other documented limits include three training seeds per learned algorithm, simplified economy and social relationships, no reservation system for crowded routes, and no consequential crime or dispatch dynamics. Future work should fix observation scaling, reevaluate current population with matched seeds, expand scenario/reward tests, and report confidence intervals with correct seed units.

Sources: README.md; docs/RL Formulation.md; docs/Testing and Experiments.md; ai_town/simulation.py:600-620; ai_town/environment.py:35-37

## Slide 23: Conclusion

AI Town implements a headless multi-agent life simulation, a PettingZoo-compatible interface, shared-actor PPO and MAPPO, reproducible training/evaluation scripts, saved outcomes, and an interactive viewer. The mathematical contracts are inspectable: 28 local features, eight high-level activities, per-citizen reward components, GAE, PPO clipping, and a MAPPO critic that predicts one value per citizen from concatenated observations.

Retained experiments show descriptive wellbeing and survival outcomes across matched environment seeds. They do not prove convergence or broad algorithmic superiority, and they describe an earlier 10-citizen configuration. The next defensible step is to correct the observation scale, regenerate results for the current 17-citizen town, and report across training and environment seeds separately.

Sources: README.md; docs/RL Formulation.md; docs/Testing and Experiments.md
